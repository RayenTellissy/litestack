I fixed it. `python3 -m tabsplit 100.00 alice bob carol` now prints `Total: $100.00`, with alice paying $33.34 and bob and carol $33.33 each. The total now matches the bill for every amount and group size I tried. That includes a name listed twice, which was a second way the same bug showed up.

**What was broken.** The receipt total could come out below the bill in two ways:
- **Uneven splits:** splitting $10.00 six ways printed `Total: $9.96`.
- **A repeated name:** `90.00 alice alice bob` printed only two lines and `Total: $60.00`.

**Root cause.** Two places in the code, each confirmed by running it:
- `allocate` in `tabsplit/money.py` rounded every share down and threw the leftover cents away. `allocate(10000, 3)` returned `[3333, 3333, 3333]`, so 1 cent was lost.
- `split_even` in `tabsplit/bill.py` stored shares keyed by name. A second "alice" replaced the first one's share, and that share was dropped.

**Fix.**
- `allocate` now gives the leftover cents one at a time to the first people listed.
- `split_even` now keeps an ordered list of (name, share) pairs, so every person entered keeps their share. `receipt` is its only caller.

**Decisions for you:**
- **Who pays the extra cents:** the first people listed. For $100.00 split three ways, alice pays $33.34. The last people, or some other spread, would also work.
- **A repeated name:** each entry gets its own line and share. Merging them into one line (alice: $66.67) or rejecting repeated names were the other options.

**Principles that changed a decision:**
- **Model the domain.** The shares are an ordered list of people where names can repeat, so I used a list of pairs instead of a dict keyed by name.
- **Fix root causes.** I fixed the share calculation itself. The Total line still adds up the printed shares rather than repeating the amount entered, which would have hidden the bug.
- **Smallest change.** Each fix changes only two to three lines of code.
- **Verifiable units.** Each fix comes after a commit with its failing test.

**Not covered.** Two input-parsing problems remain, and both existed before:
- A negative amount comes out wrong: `-10.50` prints `Total: -$9.50` (the reviewer measured this).
- Fractions of a cent are cut off: `100.009` prints `$100.00`.

They are about reading the amount, not splitting it, so I left them out. Fixing them would mean deciding between rejecting such input and rounding it. The repo has no lint or type-check setup, so neither ran.
