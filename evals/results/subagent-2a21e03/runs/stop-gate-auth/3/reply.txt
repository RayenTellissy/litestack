**My judgment on the approach**

I would not make every session last 30 days. A stolen session token would then work for a month, and nothing could revoke it. The cause is the fixed one-hour limit. A session that renews on each request would keep active users logged in without keeping idle sessions alive for a month. Remember-me would then cover the user who comes back after a break. The two fixes overlap. You likely want short renewing sessions plus remember-me, not both lasting 30 days.

**What the task needs before it is built**

A design that settles these points, then a security review:
- How the token is presented. `login` takes a password today. Logging in with a token needs its own entry point or an optional password. Returning a second token must not change what existing `login` callers get.
- Storage. Store only a hash of the remember-me token, separate from session tokens, and compare it in constant time.
- Rotation. Issue a new remember-me token on each use. Treat reuse of an old one as theft and revoke the whole series.
- Lifetime and revocation. Tokens expire after 30 days and are revoked on logout. There is also a way to revoke all of a user's tokens.
- Session policy. Pick one of three: a fixed 30 days, renewal on each request, or renewal with a fixed upper limit.
