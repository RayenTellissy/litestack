Splitting a bill no longer loses money. `python3 -m tabsplit 100.00 alice bob carol` now prints `Total: $100.00`. It's committed on the branch `fix/split-remainder`. There is no PR because the repo has no remote.

**What was broken.** When a bill didn't divide evenly, the shares added up to less than the bill. The $100.00 three-way split printed `Total: $99.99`, and $10.00 split six ways printed `Total: $9.96`.

**Root cause.** `allocate` in `tabsplit/money.py` divided the cents rounding down and gave every person the same share. The leftover cents (up to one fewer than the number of people) went to nobody. A probe showed `parse_amount("100.00")` returns 10000, which is correct, and `allocate(10000, 3)` returns `[3333, 3333, 3333]`. The receipt then printed the sum of those short shares.

**Fix.** `allocate` now uses `divmod` and gives one extra cent to each of the first few shares until the leftover is used up. Shares differ by at most one cent and always add up to the total. That is a two-line change in one function, and `split_even` is its only caller.

**Verification.** Before:
```
$ python3 -m tabsplit 100.00 alice bob carol
alice: $33.33
bob: $33.33
carol: $33.33
Total: $99.99
```

After:

- All 11 tests pass, including the two new pin tests.
- A scripted sweep made 84 real CLI runs across 12 amounts and 1 to 7 people. It checks that the total equals the amount entered, that the shares add up to it, and that no two shares differ by more than one cent. The branch had 0 mismatches and `main` had 44.
- `80.00 alice bob --tip 15` still prints `Total: $92.00`.

**Review.** The reviewer's verdict was PASS+NOTES with no findings. Its own runs matched the results above, and the pin tests fail when run against `main`'s code. Nothing needed fixing and nothing was dismissed.

**Needs your call:**
- **Who pays the extra cent.** The first people in command-line order get it, so `100.00 alice bob carol` charges alice $33.34. It could go to the last person or rotate instead.
- **Negative amounts.** Rounding down puts the larger negative share on the last people. `-100.00 a b c` gives c -$33.34. In practice the first people come out a cent worse off whether the amount is positive or negative.
- **Negative amounts are misread (also on `main`).** `-0.05` is read as +5 cents and `-100.50` as -$99.50. `parse_amount` adds the cents as a positive number, and `int("-0")` is 0. Fixing it means either handling the sign once when parsing or rejecting negative bills.
- **Extra decimals are dropped (also on `main`).** `100.005` prints `Total: $100.00`. Fixing it means rejecting or rounding digits past the cents.
- **Repeated names (also on `main`).** `100.00 alice alice bob` lists two people and prints `Total: $66.66`, because `split_even` stores shares by name. Fixing it means rejecting duplicate names or keeping one share per entry.

**Principles that changed a decision:**
- **Fix root causes.** I fixed `allocate`, where the cent first goes missing, rather than printing the parsed amount on the Total line. That would have hidden the gap while the shares still added up short.
- **Prove it works.** My first sweep read share amounts back through `parse_amount`, which gets negative amounts wrong, so it gave false readings on `main`. I rewrote it to parse with `Decimal` so the check doesn't rely on the code being tested.
- **Verifiable units.** The failing tests landed first, marked as expected failures, and the fix commit removes the mark.
- **Smallest change.** The three input bugs above each have their own cause, so I left them out of this change.
