## Fixed: splitting a bill no longer loses cents

Splitting a bill now always adds up to the amount entered. Before, any amount that didn't divide evenly lost up to (people − 1) cents. `100.00` split three ways printed `Total: $99.99`. With `--tip 15` it printed `$114.99` instead of `$115.00`.

**Root cause.** `allocate` in `tabsplit/money.py` gave everyone the rounded-down share (`total_cents // parts`) and threw away the leftover cents. Parsing and the tip were already correct: `parse_amount("100.00")` gives 10000 cents, and the loss happens only in `allocate`.

**Fix.** `allocate` now uses `divmod` and gives one leftover cent each to the first people listed:
```python
share, remainder = divmod(total_cents, parts)
return [share + 1] * remainder + [share] * (parts - remainder)
```
This also works for negative totals, because Python's `divmod` always returns a remainder between 0 and parts − 1. Before the fix, `-100.00` split three ways printed `Total: -$100.02`.

**Needs your call:**
- **Who pays the leftover cents.** The first people listed each pay one extra cent (alice pays $33.34), and the new test locks that in. I picked this because it keeps the existing list order. For a negative amount, the extra cent of credit goes to the last people listed.
- **Repeated names lose a whole share.** This is broken on `main` too, and the change doesn't touch it. `100.00 alice alice bob` prints `Total: $66.66`. `split_even` stores shares in a dict keyed by name, so the second alice overwrites the first. The fix is a product choice between rejecting duplicate names at the CLI or printing every entry in order. Either is a few lines in `tabsplit/bill.py` or `tabsplit/__main__.py`, plus a test.
- **A negative amount with cents parses wrong.** This is also broken on `main` and untouched. `-100.50` becomes -$99.50, because `parse_amount` applies the minus sign to the dollars only. The fix is either to apply the sign to the whole value or to reject negative amounts. That is a few lines in `tabsplit/money.py`, plus a test.
