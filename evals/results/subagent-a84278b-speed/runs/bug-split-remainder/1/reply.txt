The bill now always adds up to the amount entered. `python3 -m tabsplit 100.00 alice bob carol` prints `Total: $100.00`, and the extra cent goes to the first person named. This affects anyone splitting a bill that doesn't divide evenly. There is no remote, so no PR was opened. The work is on branch `fix/split-remainder`.

**What was broken.** When a bill didn't divide evenly, the leftover cents vanished from everyone's share, so the total came up short.

**Root cause.** In `tabsplit/money.py`, `allocate` gave each person the bill divided by the number of people, rounded down, and threw the remainder away. A probe confirmed this (measured). `parse_amount("100.00")` returned 10000 cents, which is correct. `allocate(10000, 3)` returned `[3333, 3333, 3333]`, which adds up to 9999.

**Fix.** `allocate` now hands one leftover cent each to the first people in the list. Shares differ by at most a cent and always add up to the amount. Two lines changed. Whoever works on this next gets a test of the exact receipt and a loop test over 2,001 amounts and 1 to 12 people. The loop test fails if any split loses or gains a cent.

**Needs your call:**
- **Who pays the extra cent.** Right now it's the first people named, in the order typed, so alice pays $33.34. For a negative amount, the extra cent of credit goes to the last person instead. Giving it to the last person or rotating it would also work. The new receipt test locks in the current choice.
- **Repeated names lose money.** `100.00 alice alice bob` prints `Total: $66.66`, and it did before this change too. Shares are stored by name, so the second "alice" overwrites the first. Fixing it means choosing between rejecting repeated names and listing each one on its own line.
- **Negative amounts with cents are misread.** `-5.50 alice bob` prints `Total: -$4.50`, as it did before. `parse_amount` adds the cents to the negative dollars instead of subtracting them. Fixing it means either reading the minus sign first or rejecting negative bills.
- **Fractions of a cent are dropped silently.** `100.005 alice bob` prints `Total: $100.00`. `parse_amount` keeps only two decimal places. Fixing it means either rejecting more than two decimals or rounding.

I left the `--tip` behavior alone. The total there is the amount plus the tip, as the README shows.
