Splitting a bill now always adds back up to the amount entered. `python3 -m tabsplit 100.00 alice bob carol` now prints `Total: $100.00`, and alice pays the extra cent. The fix is two commits on the branch `fix/allocate-remainder`. There is no PR because the repo has no remote.

**What was wrong.** Whenever the bill didn't divide evenly, everyone was charged the rounded-down share and the leftover cents vanished. With a tip the same thing happened, so `100.00` with three people and `--tip 18` came to `$117.99`.

**Root cause.** The function that divides the cents, `allocate` in `tabsplit/money.py`, rounded each share down and threw away the remainder. I checked each step in turn. Reading `100.00` gives 10000 cents, which is right. `allocate(10000, 3)` returns `[3333, 3333, 3333]`, which adds up to 9999. Printing 9999 gives `$99.99`, which is right. So the cent is lost when the bill is divided, and the receipt just adds up the shares it was given.

**Fix.** `allocate` now hands out the leftover cents one each, starting with the first person named. It is a two-line change. The receipt code is untouched.

**How I verified it.** I ran the same script on `main` and on the branch.

Before (`main`):
```
$ python3 -m tabsplit 100.00 alice bob carol
alice: $33.33
bob: $33.33
carol: $33.33
Total: $99.99
$ python3 -m tabsplit 0.05 alice bob carol
...
Total: $0.03
$ python3 -m tabsplit 100.00 alice bob carol --tip 18
...
Total: $117.99
```

After (branch):
```
$ python3 -m tabsplit 100.00 alice bob carol
alice: $33.34
bob: $33.33
carol: $33.33
Total: $100.00
$ python3 -m tabsplit 0.05 alice bob carol
alice: $0.02
bob: $0.02
carol: $0.01
Total: $0.05
$ python3 -m tabsplit 100.00 alice bob carol --tip 18
alice: $39.34
bob: $39.33
carol: $39.33
Total: $118.00
```

- **Bills that split evenly** are unchanged. `90.00` for three still gives `$30.00` each, and `80.00` for two with `--tip 15` still gives `$92.00`.
- **New tests.** I added two. One checks the full `$100.00` receipt. The other tries every total from -$5.00 to $20.00 across 1 to 12 people. It checks that the shares always add up to the total and never differ by more than one cent.
- **The tests fail without the fix.** Without the fix they failed (`'...Total: $99.99' != '...Total: $100.00'` and `-501 != -500 : allocate(-500, 3) = [-167, -167, -167]`). They went in first, marked as expected failures, and the fix commit removes the marks.
- **Full suite.** It now reports `Ran 11 tests ... OK`.
- **Nothing else is affected.** The only code that calls `allocate` is the even-split step behind the receipt.

**Review.** A separate reviewer checked the change and said PASS+NOTES. Its one finding was that my first before and after runs used different inputs, and the dropped input still loses money. I fixed that by putting the repeated-name input back and running the same script on `main` and the branch, as shown above. I dismissed nothing.

**Needs your call:**
- **Who pays the extra cent.** It goes to the first people named, so alice pays $33.34. The last person or the payer would also be reasonable. For a negative amount the larger share falls on the last names.
- **A name entered twice still loses money.** `100.00 alice alice` prints one line and `Total: $50.00`, on `main` and on the branch. The receipt is built as a lookup by name, so a repeated name overwrites the first share. That is a separate cause I left out of this fix. Fixing it means either keeping a list of people or rejecting repeated names, and that is a product choice.
- **Some amounts are read wrong before the split.** `-5.50` is read as -$4.50, and `100.005` is cut to $100.00. Both come from the amount-reading code, a separate cause also left out. Fixing it means applying the minus sign to the cents too, and deciding whether to reject or round more than two decimals.

**Principles that changed a decision:**
- **Fix root causes.** I fixed the division instead of printing the entered amount on the Total line, which would have hidden that the shares don't add up.
- **Smallest change.** The repeated-name and amount-reading bugs stay out of this change and are listed above.
- **Test behavior.** The tests check the printed receipt and the shares returned, not how the code works inside.
- **Verifiable units.** The failing tests were committed before the fix.
- **Prove it works.** After the review, the before and after runs come from the same script.
