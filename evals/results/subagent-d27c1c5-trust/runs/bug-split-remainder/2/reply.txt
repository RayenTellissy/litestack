**What was broken.** When a bill does not split evenly, the leftover cents were lost. The receipt total came out lower than the amount entered.

**Root cause.** `allocate` in `tabsplit/money.py` gave everyone the rounded-down share (`total // parts`) and threw away the leftover cents. I checked each step separately (measured). Reading the amount, adding the tip, and formatting were all correct. `allocate(10000, 3)` returned `[3333, 3333, 3333]`, which adds up to 9999. The bug has been there since the first commit, so nothing recent broke it (measured with `git log -L`).

**Fix.** `allocate` now uses `divmod` and gives one extra cent to each of the first people until the leftover runs out. Two lines changed. Shares always add up to the total, and no two differ by more than one cent. A new test that loops over many inputs keeps both of those true from now on.

**Needs your call.**
1. Who pays the extra cent. Right now the people listed first pay it, so alice pays $33.34. You could put it on the last person or rotate it instead.
2. Repeated names. Shares are stored by name, so a second `alice` replaces the first and her share drops out of the total. You could reject repeated names with an error, or print each person on their own line.
3. More than two decimal places. Only the first two digits after the point are kept, so `100.005` becomes $100.00. You could reject the input or round it.
4. Negative amounts with cents. The cents are added to the negative dollars instead of subtracted, so `-5.50` becomes -$4.50 and `-0.50` becomes $0.50. You could reject negative amounts or keep the minus sign on the cents too.

**Principles that changed a decision.**
- Fix root causes. I fixed `allocate` instead of making the receipt print the amount entered. Printing the amount entered would have hidden shares that do not add up.
- Smallest change. I left the other three bad inputs out of this change. Each has its own cause and needs a choice from you.
- Test behavior. The first new test checks the Total line a user sees. The loop test checks what `allocate` returns, not how it works inside.
- Prove it works. My first try at the other three inputs failed with an argument error. I checked my own command before blaming the app. The shell had passed the arguments wrong. I reran them from a script.
