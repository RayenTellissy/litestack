**What was broken.** When a bill didn't divide evenly, it came up short. Each person was charged the rounded-down share and the leftover cents were thrown away. With 3 people that is up to 2 cents.

**Root cause.** `allocate` in `tabsplit/money.py` divided and rounded down, then returned that share for everyone. I checked each step for `100.00` split 3 ways. Reading the amount gave 10000 cents. Adding a 0% tip gave 10000. Printing 10000 gave `$100.00`. Only `allocate(10000, 3)` was wrong. It returned `[3333, 3333, 3333]`, which adds up to 9999.

**Fix.** `allocate` now hands out the leftover cents one at a time, starting with the first person. Shares always add up to the total and never differ by more than one cent. It's two changed lines in one file. Its only caller is `split_even`.

**Needs your call**
- **Duplicate names.** `100.00 alice alice bob` prints `Total: $66.66`. This is the same "loses money" symptom from a separate cause. `split_even` stores shares by name, so a repeated name overwrites the earlier share. The fix is either to reject repeated names when the command is read, or to list each one separately. It's a small change in `tabsplit/bill.py` or `tabsplit/__main__.py`.
- **Negative amounts.** `-1.50 alice bob` prints `Total: -$0.50`. The reviewer found `-0.50` prints `Total: $0.50`. `parse_amount` applies the minus sign to the dollars but not the cents. The fix is either to reject negative amounts or to read the sign separately. Which one depends on whether a negative bill should be allowed at all.
- **More than two decimals.** `100.999 alice bob` prints `Total: $100.99`, because `parse_amount` drops extra digits. The fix is to reject such amounts or round them. You pick which.
- **Who pays the extra cent.** I chose the first people listed, so alice pays $33.34. Your request didn't say. For a negative amount the larger share goes to the last person instead (reviewer measured: `-100.00 alice bob carol` charges carol -$33.34). The totals still match either way.
